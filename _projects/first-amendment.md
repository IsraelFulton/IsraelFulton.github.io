## The question

*Truthful Representation: First Amendment Principles, Campaign Finance, and Capital Markets* examines a tension between two settings where information matters: electoral politics and securities markets.

The paper asks how the treatment of corporate political spending relates to the informational conditions that help people make decisions, while preserving political expression.

## My approach

I bring First Amendment theory into conversation with the economics of information and securities regulation. A stylized model explores how concentrated political spending might affect electoral outcomes through the distribution of information.

The paper uses that framework to examine possible disclosure, shareholder-authorization, and anti-fraud mechanisms. These are proposals developed in the draft, rather than claims about demonstrated policy effects.

## Scope and limitations

This is a theoretical and normative research paper. The model is an illustration of a mechanism, not an empirical estimate of the effect of campaign spending. The draft's legal arguments and policy proposals remain open to criticism and revision.
